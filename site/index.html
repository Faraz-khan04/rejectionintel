How it works

Three steps. You only do the first one.

1

You send us your applications

A list of where you applied: company, job title, date, and what happened. Upload a spreadsheet, paste from Google Sheets, or type them in one at a time. Include the job descriptions if you kept them. That is how we find the skills you kept getting asked for. We check every row and tell you if any look wrong.

2

We do the maths. No AI touches a number

We read your resume and every job description. Then we work out three things: how closely you matched each role, how often you heard back, and how far off those numbers could be given how many applications you have sent. It is ordinary arithmetic — the kind you could redo in a spreadsheet if you wanted to check us.

3

You get answers, labelled by how sure we are

Before we call anything a pattern, we check whether bad luck could explain it. If it could, we drop it. Only then does the AI turn what is left into plain English, using only the numbers we already worked out. It is not allowed to come up with its own.

The math

Why we don't sell resume A/B testing

Everyone asks for it, and it looks great in a demo: send Resume A to ten jobs, Resume B to ten more, declare a winner. Here's the problem. If you normally hear back from 1 in 20 applications, proving one resume is genuinely twice as good takes about 432 applications with each version.

Check us

Everything above, and how to prove it yourself

We say we do not overclaim. So do not take that on faith. Every claim below comes with a way to check it.

We almost never invent a pattern

Test something twenty-five times and you'll find a couple of "real" results by pure luck. We correct for that. Run against 300 simulated people whose outcomes were pure chance, we reported a strong finding 0.02 times per person. Without the correction it's roughly twenty times higher.

Check it: run python -m tests.test_pipeline. The false-positive simulation is in there and prints its own result.

The AI can't make up a number

Every figure in your report is worked out in ordinary code first. The AI only turns those figures into sentences. Its output is checked back against them. If it writes a number that isn't in your data, that draft is thrown away before you see it.

Check it: the same check refuses a resume suggestion containing a metric you never wrote — ask for a rewrite and you'll get a blank to fill, never an invented percentage.

We say "we don't know" out loud

Telling two resume versions apart reliably takes about 432 applications with each one. Below that we show you both numbers and refuse to name a winner — even when one looks twice as good. Most tools would call it.

Check it: upload data with two resume versions. The panel says how many more applications an honest answer would need.

Sharing can't leak who you applied to

The share card carries counts and rates, nothing else. Before it's built we check it against every company name, job title and rejection quote in your own report. If anything survives, you get no card rather than a leaky one.

Check it: run python -m tests.test_share_redaction. On our sample it checks against 232 identifying strings and proves the check isn't a no-op.

No jargon, enforced rather than intended

Statistical language leaks through error messages and fallback text, not just the parts someone remembers to edit. So there's a dictionary of the terms and an automatic check that scans what reaches your screen for anything we forgot.

Check it: read the report. If you find a p-value, a confidence interval or a variable name anywhere in it, that's a bug and we want to hear about it.

Questions

The ones worth answering honestly

Can you tell me why a specific company rejected me?

No. Unless they wrote it down, nobody can — and most don't. What we can do is find patterns across your own applications and show you how confident those patterns are. If an employer did write a reason, we quote it back to you as theirs, not as ours.

Why do I need 25 applications before anything happens?

Below that, any pattern we found would be noise wearing a suit. You'll still see your funnel and per-application fit scores from your very first upload — we just won't dress up chance as insight. The counter tells you how many more you need.

What counts as a rejection if they never replied?

It doesn't. Silence is recorded as ghosted, not rejected, and applications still inside the response window are excluded from the statistics entirely — counting them would bias results against your most recent applications, which have had less time to come back.

Do you tell me my chances will go from 18% to 34%?

Never. To say that, we'd have to replay your job search with one thing changed — same you, same roles, different resume. Nobody has that. Any product quoting you a predicted lift is quoting you a number it made up.

Does an AI write my report?

It writes the sentences, and only the sentences. Every figure is computed first in ordinary code. The writing step receives those figures and nothing else, and its output is checked against them — if it produces a number that isn't in the source data, that draft is rejected before you ever see it.

Do you need access to my email?

Not today. The beta is CSV only. Inbox parsing would make the data far richer and it's on the roadmap, but it's a heavy permission to ask for before we've shown you the analysis is worth having.
