Is it the market, or is it you?

On your own you cannot tell. Twelve rejections could mean your resume is weak, or it could mean nobody in your field is getting replies this quarter — and the difference decides whether you rewrite anything.

Pool your applications with a bootcamp cohort, a university class, or a few friends job hunting at the same time, and the answer becomes visible: the same analysis, run across everyone at once.

You will need an account first. A guest session has nowhere to belong, so it cannot be counted as a member of a group. Sign in or create one — it takes a minute, and you keep working as a guest until you do.

Start a group

You get a six-character code to share. Everyone who joins with it is counted, and you can watch the group fill up before any report appears.

Join a group

Someone running a group will have sent you a code. Joining adds your applications to the totals — never to anyone's screen.

What everyone else can see about you

Nothing. Not the companies you applied to, not the roles, not how many times you were turned down. A group report shows totals only — how the group as a whole is doing.

It also stays locked until eight people have joined. In a group of three, a total is a person: subtract your own numbers and you have someone else's. Eight is the smallest size where that stops working.
